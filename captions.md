## Thu Dec 3, 08:30 ET · Explainer
**Will cheaper AI mean more AI? The Jevons paradox**  
Folder: `posts/2026-12-03-jevons-paradox/` (7 images)

```
Will cheaper AI mean more AI?

In 1865, economist William Stanley Jevons argued that more efficient steam engines wouldn’t save Britain’s coal. Cheaper power would invite new uses, and total coal use would climb. That idea is now called the Jevons paradox.

It came roaring back in January 2025, when Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella cited it the day DeepSeek’s low-cost model shook AI stocks.

Economists add a caveat: efficiency usually produces a partial rebound in demand, not a full backfire.

Sources: NPR Planet Money (2025), Fortune (Jan 27, 2025), Northeastern University (2025)

#ai #economics #startups #tech

## Fri Dec 4, 08:30 ET · Founder story
**Microsoft’s first product was sold before it existed**  
Folder: `posts/2026-12-04-microsoft-basic/` (7 images)

```
Microsoft’s first product was sold before it existed.

In early 1975, Bill Gates and Paul Allen told MITS, maker of the Altair 8800, that they had BASIC software for its new computer. They didn’t. With no Altair of their own, they wrote it on a simulator Allen built on Harvard’s PDP-10, with Monte Davidoff writing the math routines.

Allen wrote the last missing piece, the loader, on the plane to the demo. It worked, MITS licensed it, and Microsoft was officially founded on April 4, 1975.

Sources: Computer History Museum, The New Stack, Wikipedia (Altair BASIC)

## Thu Dec 24, 10:00 ET · Explainer
**Networks grow by squares: Metcalfe’s law**  
Folder: `posts/2026-12-24-metcalfes-law/` (7 images)

```
Why do networks get more valuable as they grow?

Around 1980, Ethernet co-inventor Bob Metcalfe sketched a simple idea: the possible connections in a network grow roughly with the square of its users. Two users share one link. Twelve can form 66.

His real point was critical mass. A network has to get big enough to be worth what it costs.

George Gilder named it Metcalfe’s law in 1993. Critics say the true curve is flatter, since not every connection is equally valuable. Metcalfe later received the 2022 Turing Award for Ethernet.

Sources: IEEE Spectrum (2006), Wikipedia

#networkeffects #startups #tech #venturecapital
```
